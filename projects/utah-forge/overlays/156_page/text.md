Milford valley, Beaver County, Utah. The Mineral Mountains rise to the east, alluvial flats run west to town. The Utah FORGE footprint sits between them: a ~5 km² parcel of high desert inside Utah’s Renewable Energy Corridor.

Utah FORGE is a US Department of Energy field laboratory run by the University of Utah, built to develop Enhanced Geothermal Systems: extracting heat from hot, dry granite with no natural fluid pathways. The site was chosen for its crystalline basement, 175 to 225°C reservoir, 1.5 to 4 km depth, and low seismic risk.

In conventional reports - A state locator map and a paragraph of prose. You learn where it is, never what it feels like or why it was picked.

Our approach - Open on the real ground first. The viewer lands in the valley, reads the basin-and-range setting, then descends into the data as the layers build.

The difference - The audience starts oriented. By the time the subsurface appears, they know what sits above it and why this desert was chosen.

Why this site (for the demo) - One of the best publicly documented subsurface datasets in the world. Every layer is released CC-BY 4.0 through the DOE Geothermal Data Repository, letting us show the full Geovox method on real, citable data.
